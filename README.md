Complete **pure SQL** solution for the AnalytixLabs **Retail Customer Analysis** case study.  
All 20 business questions solved using clean, efficient, production-ready SQL queries (no Python/Pandas).

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

A retail store wants to deeply understand customer behavior, product performance, store efficiency, and revenue patterns using 3 years of Point-of-Sale (POS) data.

**Goal**: Write **one single SQL query per question** to derive actionable insights from Customer, Transactions, and Product Category tables.

---

## 📁 Datasets

| Table                | Description                              | Rows     |
|----------------------|------------------------------------------|----------|
| `Customer`           | Customer demographics (DOB, Gender, City)| 5,648    |
| `Transactions`       | All customer transactions (2011–2014)    | 23,053   |
| `prod_cat_info`      | Product hierarchy (Category & Sub-category)| 23     |

**Relationship**: `Transactions` links to `Customer` via `cust_id` and to `prod_cat_info` via `prod_cat_code` + `prod_subcat_code`.

---

## ✅ All 20 Business Questions Solved (Pure SQL)

### Data Preparation & Understanding
1. Total number of rows in each of the 3 tables  
2. Total number of transactions that have a return (negative `total_amt`)  
3. Convert all date variables into valid date format  
4. Time range of transaction data (in days, months, and years)  
5. Which product category does the sub-category “DIY” belong to?

### Data Analysis
1. Most frequently used channel (`Store_type`) for transactions  
2. Count of Male and Female customers  
3. City with maximum customers + percentage contribution  
4. Number of sub-categories under the **Books** category  
5. Maximum quantity of products ever ordered  
6. Net total revenue from **Electronics** and **Books** categories  
7. Customers with **>10 unique transactions** (excluding returns)  
8. Combined revenue from **Electronics** & **Clothing** from **Flagship stores**  
9. Total revenue from **Male** customers in **Electronics** category (by sub-category)  
10. Percentage of sales & returns by product sub-category (Top 5 by sales)  
11. Net total revenue generated by customers aged **25–35** in the **last 30 days** from the maximum transaction date  
12. Product category with **maximum value of returns** in the last 3 months  
13. Store type that sells the **maximum products** — by sales value **and** by quantity  
14. Categories where **average revenue > overall average** revenue  
15. Average and total revenue by sub-category for the **top 5 categories** by quantity sold

---

## 🔍 Key Business Insights (Derived from SQL Queries)

- **Flagship stores** generate the highest revenue both by value and quantity  
- **Electronics** and **Books** together contribute the maximum revenue  
- **City Code 4** has the highest customer base (~10.5%)  
- Males dominate spending in **Electronics**; Females prefer **Clothing** & **Footwear**  
- Customers aged **25–35** are high-value — significant revenue in short windows  
- High number of **returns** — opportunity to reduce return rate  
- Top-performing sub-categories identified for targeted promotions

## 🛠️ SQL Features Used

- Complex `JOIN`s (INNER + LEFT)
- Date functions (`TO_DATE`, `DATE_TRUNC`, `EXTRACT`, `DATEDIFF`)
- Window functions (`ROW_NUMBER()`, `RANK()`)
- CTEs for readability and modularity
- Aggregations, `GROUP BY`, `HAVING`, `CASE` statements
- Sub-queries and correlated queries
- Percentage calculations and top-N logic

**Tested on**: PostgreSQL, MySQL, and SQLite
